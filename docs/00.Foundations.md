# Foundations

Let's try a thought experiment.

First, I'm going to assume that you've used [ChatGPT](https://openai.com/chatgpt/), which is currently the most popular AI application.

While it is out of date, the [GPT-3.5 vs GPT-4: Comparing OpenAI's Language Models](https://www.auberginesolutions.com/blog/gpt-3-5-vs-gpt-4-an-in-depth-analysis-of-openais-language-models/) article compares the functionality of two versions. 

But imagine that you'd like to use a GPT to write an article on gardening, which would you choose? That article doesn't help much.

You are now encountering the the exploration vs. exploitation trade-off dilemma:

* Exploration: Trying out different GPTs to discover their performance characteristics. This might involve testing a new GPT that you've never used before.  
* Exploitation: Using the GPT that you currently believe performs the best, based on past experiences.

Balancing these two strategies is crucial. If you focus solely on exploitation, you might miss out on discovering a new GPT that could perform even better. Conversely, if you spend too much time exploring, you may waste resources on inferior GPT.

Consider also that you could use a totally different system from ChatGPT, this dilemma could be generalized to using different models even within ChatGPT.

Let's use some example data to illustrate how you might approach this problem.

NOTE: If you find that the text below is difficult to comprehend, consider the same problem described as choosing a restaurant in this [Data science concepts video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e3L4VocZnnQ).

## **Initial Trials**

You start by testing each model on a small set of articles to get an initial idea of their performance.

### **Test Data**

You select 3 gardening articles and use AI to generate summaries using each model.

### **Performance Metrics**

You decide to measure performance using a simple scoring system from 0 to 100 based on factors like accuracy, coherence, and readability.

### **Initial Results**

| Model | Article 1 | Article 2 | Article 3 | Average Score |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| GPT-3 | 85 | 80 | 90 | 85 |
| GPT-3.5 | 88 | 85 | 87 | 86.7 |
| BERT | 75 | 78 | 80 | 77.7 |

* **GPT-3** average score: **85**  
* **GPT-3.5** average score: **86.7**  
* **BERT** average score: **77.7**

Based on these initial tests, **GPT-3.5** seems to perform the best.

## **The Greedy Approach**

One simple strategy is to always choose the model with the highest average score so far.

* **Exploitation Only**: From now on, always use **GPT-3.5**.

**Problem**: What if the initial tests were not representative? Maybe **GPT-3** performs better on different types of articles, or perhaps **BERT** excels in certain contexts.

## **Introducing Exploration: The ε-Greedy Algorithm**

To balance exploration and exploitation, you can use the **ε-greedy algorithm**.

### **How It Works**

* **Set ε (epsilon)**: A small probability value between 0 and 1, say **ε \= 0.2** (20%).  
* **Decision Rule**:  
  * With **80% probability (1 \- ε)**: Exploit by choosing the model with the highest average score (**GPT-3.5**).  
  * With **20% probability (ε)**: Explore by randomly selecting any of the models.

### **Implementing the Algorithm**

Let's say you have to generate summaries for 10 more articles. For each article:

1. **Generate a Random Number** between 0 and 1\.  
2. **If** the number is less than 0.2, **explore**:  
   * Randomly pick **GPT-3** or **BERT**.  
3. **Else**, **exploit**:  
   * Use **GPT-3.5**.

You could roll a dice and say if it lands on 1 that represents the exploit step (actually 16.67% chance).

### **Simulated Results**

Assume the following additional performance scores:

| Article | Model Used | Score | Updated Average |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 4 | GPT-3.5 | 89 | GPT-3.5: 87 |
| 5 | BERT | 82 | BERT: 79 |
| 6 | GPT-3.5 | 90 | GPT-3.5: 88 |
| 7 | GPT-3 | 88 | GPT-3: 86 |
| 8 | GPT-3.5 | 85 | GPT-3.5: 87.6 |
| 9 | GPT-3.5 | 91 | GPT-3.5: 88.4 |
| 10 | GPT-3 | 92 | GPT-3: 88.7 |
| 11 | GPT-3.5 | 87 | GPT-3.5: 88.1 |
| 12 | GPT-3.5 | 89 | GPT-3.5: 88.2 |
| 13 | BERT | 80 | BERT: 79.5 |

### **Observations**

* **GPT-3**'s updated average score is now **88.7**, slightly higher than **GPT-3.5**'s **88.2**.  
* Through exploration, we've discovered that **GPT-3** performs better on these new articles.

### **Adjusting Your Strategy**

With the updated averages:

* **Exploitation** now favors **GPT-3**.  
* Continue using the ε-greedy algorithm to balance exploration and exploitation.

## Conclusion

Artificial intelligence is a mathematical technique that produces algorithms and models capable of performing tasks that typically require human intelligence, such as learning, reasoning, problem-solving, perception, and language understanding.

AI is not a replacement for human intelligence but rather a tool that complements and enhances our abilities.

AI can be used for a wide range of applications, including:

* **Automating Routine Tasks**: Streamlining processes in industries like manufacturing, finance, and customer service.  
* **Data Analysis and Insights**: Analyzing large datasets to uncover patterns, trends, and make predictions.  
* **Healthcare Advancements**: Assisting in diagnosing diseases, personalized medicine, and drug discovery.  
* **Natural Language Processing**: Enabling language translation, sentiment analysis, and chatbots for customer support.  
* **Computer Vision**: Recognizing and interpreting visual information for applications like facial recognition and autonomous vehicles.  
* **Recommendation Systems**: Personalizing content in platforms like e-commerce sites, streaming services, and social media.  
* **Robotics**: Powering intelligent robots for tasks ranging from household chores to complex industrial operations.  
* **Education**: Providing personalized learning experiences and tutoring through adaptive learning systems.  
* **Environmental Monitoring**: Predicting weather patterns, monitoring climate change, and managing natural resources.  
* **Security and Fraud Detection**: Identifying fraudulent activities and enhancing cybersecurity measures.

The example above is an example of the [multi-armed bandit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-armed_bandit) concept and is the sort of basic computer science that you will learn about in this course.
