# Caveats of AI

As you have seen from the previous lessons AI is a complex field yet the potential use cases for AI are endless. However, there are some caveats to using AI.

The misuse of AI can create potential ethical dilemmas, particularly for businesses. If generating content with the intention of reuse or distribution there is uncertainty over who owns the content. There is still lots of discussion about copyright laws when the content is AI generated. Technically the work is not yours despite the fact that the prompt which inspired the asset belongs to you.

Educators are understandably concerned about the implications of AI, however if leveraged correctly AI can be beneficial to students. There is a fear that AI will be abused by students when submitting assignments. It should be noted that plagiarism checks for AI generated content do exist. AI watermarking is the process of hiding identifying signals into a model's output. Specialised algorithms are used to detect these markers. 

Over reliance on chat bots instead of a search engine to quickly provide answers is unadvised. Despite AI engineers making every effort to ensure the accuracy of models, no model can achieve 100% accuracy. AI models may hallucinate. An hallucination is a term used when the model invents an answer to a user's prompt. If unsure about the validity of a response from a model it can be difficult to track the decision making process and sources used by the model to come to a conclusion. This problem is referred to as explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) and is being actively worked on to ensure greater trust in AI models. This is particularly important where AI is used to make important decisions such as in the legal system. There have been reports of models issuing biased recommendations.

Many are unaware of the price of AI in terms of time, cost and energy. More and more servers in datacenters are being set aside for dedicated AI model training. It can take hours or even weeks of continuous training to prepare a well trained model. This constant use of servers has increased energy consumption within data centers. Purchasing the latest and most powerful GPUs for training is expensive. What's worse is that the GPU improvements quickly make existing hardware obsolete. This puts even greater pressure on the maintainers of data centers.
